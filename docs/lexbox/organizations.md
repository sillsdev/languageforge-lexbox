An organization groups the projects and people that belong together: a language program,
an institution, a partner organization. Find yours under **Organizations** in the top bar.

Organizations are created by the Lexbox team. Write to
[admin@languagedepot.org](mailto:admin@languagedepot.org) if yours needs one.

## Who can see what

Everything on lexbox.org needs a login. Logged in, what you see of an organization depends
on your relation to it:

| | Anyone logged in | Organization member | Organization admin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The organization's name, member count and project count | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Projects marked **Not confidential** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Other projects | Only ones you are a member of | Only ones you are a member of | Yes, all |
| Member list | Admins' names only | All names and roles | Names, roles and email or login |
| Project data (Send/Receive, FieldWorks Lite) | Only projects you are a member of | Only projects you are a member of | Yes, all projects |

Admins' names are shown to everyone on purpose, so people can find who to ask about
joining.

:::note
Being a member of an organization gives no access to any project's data. You get that only
from a [project role](/lexbox/manage-members#roles), or by being an organization admin.

Putting a confidential project in an organization does not expose it. It stays hidden from
everyone except its members, the organization's admins and the Lexbox team.
:::

A project marked **Not confidential** is open for anyone logged in to find: its name, code,
description and history (including who sent changes). That is what the setting is for:
letting others discover related projects and ask to join. Its member list stays private. If
that is more than you want,
[mark the project confidential](/lexbox/manage-members#confidential-projects).

Nobody can look up another user's email address on lexbox.org, not even organization
admins. To add someone, you need their email or login from them.

## Roles

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| **Member** | See the organization's projects marked **Not confidential** (plus their own) and choose **Ask to join project** on them; add projects they manage |
| **Admin** | Everything a member can, plus rename the organization, add and remove people, and manage every project the organization owns |

An admin counts as a manager on all of the organization's projects, confidential ones
included, without being listed as a member. Join requests still go to the project's
managers, not to organization admins.

## Managing

- **Add Member** adds a person by email or login. If they don't have an account yet, tick
  **Invite** to send them an invitation email. Admins can also create an account for
  someone with **Create User**.
- **Bulk Add Members** takes a list of logins or emails. Unlike the project version, it
  does not create accounts for people who don't have one.
- Anyone can **Leave organization** (on the **Settings** tab), except the last admin, who
  must first add another admin. Leaving the organization does not remove you from its
  projects; use **Leave Project** on each project page for that.

## Projects

- **Add My Projects** on the organization page lists the projects you manage; pick the
  ones to add. You can also use **Add Organization** on a project page, or choose the
  organization on the form when [creating a project](/lexbox/host-a-project).
- Organization admins and the project's managers can remove a project from the
  organization. That only unlinks it: project members keep their roles, organization
  admins lose their access, and a project marked **Not confidential** stays visible to
  anyone logged in.
